With most goods a larger pack works out cheaper. With Telegram Stars it **does not** — and the services say so themselves.

## Packages and the per-unit price

Look at the "price per Star" column: it is a single number for each service, because **pack size does not move it**. Divide any package total by its quantity and the same figure comes back.

## Why there is no discount

Both services publish a **single per-unit price** on their own pages — that is their stated condition, not our inference. The reason is straightforward: a service buys Stars in advance and resells them, and its costs do not fall meaningfully with quantity, so there is no basis for a volume discount.

The practical result: **buy 50 or buy 5000, one Star costs the same.**

## What to look at instead

One thing moves the price — **which service you buy from**. The two published per-unit rates differ, and that gap multiplies across every Star: the larger the quantity, the wider the total difference between services.

So on a large purchase, compare services rather than packages. The pages that help: [Stars prices](/en/telegram-stars), the [calculator](/en/kalkulyator) and the [service ranking](/en/reyting/telegram-stars).

## How much to buy

With no volume discount, buying "for later" makes no financial sense. Buy what you need.

Telegram Stars sit on the balance without expiry, so a surplus is not lost — but that is convenience, not saving. If the amount you want is not listed, the per-unit price is unchanged, so work the total out in the [calculator](/en/kalkulyator) and confirm it in the bot.

Whatever the quantity, the rule holds: **no service needs your Telegram password, SMS code or 2FA password.**
